## 1. The Concept

Each player takes control of an abstract group of randomly generated adventurers in their own version or dimension of the town of Oakhurst. There is no limit to the number of characters one player can have, and there need be no solid in-universe connection between all of the characters of one player (though this can be the case if desired). Character generation need not be completely random, but all characters start at level one with random stats, race, and class as defined later in this document.

The town starts as a small thorp and can be built out into a sprawling metropolis over time through this system of metaprogression. Progression is generally purchased using treasure and connections gathered on adventures with one's characters. In short, after acquiring loot from an adventure, an adventurer can choose to buy gear to supply themselves for the next adventure, or they can put aside money to invest in the town of Oakhurst itself, allowing their future characters to start with benefits such as starting with a higher level, a specific class/race, magic items, and anything else that the player and DM can think of. Pricing guidelines as well as example progression trees will be provided later in this document.

## 2. Character Creation

### 2.1 Generating Statistics

Roll 4d6 and drop the lowest die. All stats are placed on the character sheet in order. The first roll is strength, second is dexterity, etc. until all stats are filled out. They cannot be moved around after being rolled, and there is no minimum or maximum for any individual statistic or the total stat array.

This method will generate non-viable characters and is intended to. Any character that a player does not wish to play can simply be abandoned, but the spirit of the game is to work with what you rolled, within reason.

> **Example:** Jeremy rolls a 7 for intelligence but in step 3 of character generation, he rolls a wizard and so, with rules as written, his character can not even cast any wizard spells. It is up to Jeremy if he wants to scrap this character and start over, or if he wants to see how far he can get with this doomed character.

Character generation is intended to be very quick, less than fifteen minutes, so a slain character can easily be replaced within a gaming session, perhaps even multiple times.

### 2.2 Race Choices

Roll 1d8, race is chosen by the roll. Players must play the primary race selected by die roll; subraces can be chosen by the player if unlocked.

> **Example:** If a player rolls gnome (3), they could select rock or forest gnome (no level adjustment), but Svirfneblin would need to be unlocked for the player to choose it.

Which races are "free" is determined by the DM, but a "free" race will normally have no level adjustment and match the subtype rolled in the table below.

| Roll | Race |
|---|---|
| 1 | Dwarf |
| 2 | Elf |
| 3 | Gnome |
| 4 | Half-elf |
| 5 | Half-orc |
| 6 | Halfling |
| 7 | Human |
| 8* | Player's choice |

\* The player may choose any race above or from one of the races unlocked through an MM (see Section 3).

### 2.3 Class Choices

Roll 1d12, class chosen by the roll. Players must play the class selected by die roll at level 1, but may multiclass into any other class afterwards (assuming they qualify). As with race, any modifications to the class may be discussed with the DM, some of which may require an MM to use.

| Roll | Class |
|---|---|
| 1 | Barbarian |
| 2 | Bard |
| 3 | Cleric |
| 4 | Druid |
| 5 | Fighter |
| 6 | Monk |
| 7 | Paladin |
| 8 | Ranger |
| 9 | Rogue |
| 10 | Sorcerer |
| 11 | Wizard |
| 12* | Player's choice |

\* The player may choose any base 3.5e PHB class or any others which they have unlocked through an MM (see Section 3) as their starting class.

## 3. Town Progression

This progression is intended generally to be described narratively in-world as the town being physically or metaphysically expanded in some way.

> **Example:** If the desired growth option is "all of my characters may begin as fighters," perhaps this option would be called "Barracks"/"Fighter's Guild" (physical) to imply that such a building now exists in the player's town, or "Martial Traditions" (metaphysical) to imply that the town has developed a baseline propensity for martial training.

Multiple players need not be of the same level nor follow any sort of continuity with each other. A character may go on an adventure with any other characters chosen by the players present, but generally a character stays on the same adventure/dungeon until it is abandoned in-game or they are killed.

> **Example:** JJ and Kraig both show up to play D&D one day. JJ has a level 3 character and Kraig wants to roll up a new one. If there are no complaints from Kraig or the DM, JJ could use his level 3 character if that character is not already on an adventure. Otherwise, JJ rolls up a new character with Kraig, and they set off together. For future sessions, Kraig and JJ can decide between sessions if they want to keep their characters together (they continue their adventure where they left off) or if they want to separate (Kraig's character could delve deeper by himself).

Progression options, called **Metaprogression Modifiers (MM)**, are split between two types:

### 3.1 Adventurer Trait Tokens (ATT)

Single-use "coupons" which apply to one new character whenever desired and are consumed. Essentially, the tavern of Oakhurst attracts an adventurer, and this one adventurer has the purchased trait.

> **Example:** After an adventure with one of his characters, "Shiv," JJ spends 1000gp of his character's wealth to buy an ATT that allows him to start any one new character with 1000xp (lvl 2). Any future characters after the ATT is used do not gain this 1000xp at the start of their career. Perhaps news of Shiv's exploits have reached the ears of a traveler, or he has managed to get his cousin to move to Oakhurst.

### 3.2 Town Progression Option (TPO)

Permanent upgrades, purchased all at once or incrementally, which may apply to all future characters for this player. Essentially, your new character has been affected by the town in some way and can start with those benefits. This can be as simple as "my character is part of the fighter's guild," or as abstract as would make sense for the character's backstory.

> **Example:** After making it back to town from adventuring for a while with Shiv, JJ spends 5,000gp of his character's wealth in addition to 3,000gp which was spent from a previous adventure with Shiv and 2,000gp which was spent by a different character of JJ's (10,000gp total) to purchase a TPO that allows him to start all future characters with 1000xp (lvl 2). Perhaps the town has gained a reputation that attracts stronger adventurers, or perhaps the town has built their own small adventuring guild.

As determined collaboratively by Player and DM, TPOs may have a progression tree that allows them to be expanded upon as the player invests more wealth in a particular area. This may be simple and linear, such as spending more money to increase the starting level of new characters (perhaps the reputation grows or the guild expands), or it could be as complex as a branching net of upgrades with prerequisites and restrictions tied to the growth of the TPO (perhaps before the adventurer's guild can provide lvl 3 characters, other upgrades such as "lvl 2 characters" or "lvl 2 starting gold" must be purchased). These can be as simple or as complex as the player and DM decide.

## 4. Buildings

### 4.1 Class Buildings

Purchasing these builds unlocks certain classes at character creation instead of having them randomly assigned.

| Building | Unlocks |
|---|---|
| Temple | Divine classes pertaining to deities (clerics, paladins, favored souls, etc.) |
| Mages Guild | Arcane classes (sorcerers, warlocks, wizards, etc.) |
| Wilderness Enclave | Nature-based classes (rangers, druids, barbarians) |
| Tavern | Skill-based classes (bards, rogues) |
| Training Yard | Martial classes (fighter, monk) |

### 4.2 Race Buildings

**Sample MM (ATT or TPO): Creature Dwelling**

Your Oakhurst has made connections or has proximity to a certain race's dwelling. This could be a gold dwarf stronghold that starts to trade with the town, or a gnoll tribe living in the wilderness. The specifics may be decided by player and DM collaboratively, but the result remains the same: you may choose this race when making any new characters, assuming your character starts with the requisite level adjustment for the race chosen.

Prices for each dwelling should be decided by the level adjustment. A sun elf with no level adjustment might be worth 1,000gp, whereas a goliath with 1 level adjustment might be 10,000gp.

An ATT version of this TPO might be purchased for a tenth of the price — 100gp and 1,000gp respectively.
